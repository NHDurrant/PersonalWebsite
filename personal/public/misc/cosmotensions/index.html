Cosmological Tensions: A Tiered Introduction

This is meant to be a brief overview of the Cosmological Tensions, for the non-expert. It will be segmented into levels, each of which are slightly more technical than the last. Those with no expertise or prior knowlegde of Cosmology should work through this for as long as they feel comfortable.

This is predominantly a resource for my family and friends, whom are interested in what I will (potentially) pursue for my PhD starting in 2027. I hope to add more levels over time.

Level 0: Introduction

Let us first inspect the titular phrase itself:

Cosmological Tensions

This is made up of two words:

  • Cosmological - relating to the Cosmos (the universe) or the scientific study of it (Cosmology).
  • Tensions - some disagreements within the cosmological framework.

It is worth spending a short while thinking about Cosmology as a field, before burrowing down to the main topic of this article. Cosmology is the study of the Universe, based on the assumption that it is somehow predictable, and that humans (and no doubt, in the future, AI) can understand the inner workings. Like an automotive engineer can understand the workings of a car made up of many smaller components working in harmony, a Cosmologist believes he can understand the workings of the large system (the Universe) from understanding the smaller components (the laws of Physics).

Science and Mathematics gives us as a species the power to make predictions about the world we live in, and then test them to check for success. A prediction or theory is classed as successful if it is verified (to a certain confidence level – think ‘beyond plausible doubt’) by experiment or observation.

Cosmology has given rise to many such ‘successful’ theories. An example is the prediction of Black Holes (of Stephen Hawking, and Interstellar fame) that we then verified by looking at orbits of distant stars.

In essence, the Cosmological Tensions are a disagreement in observations. Looking at different things has given us different measurements for the exact same value. This is worrying because it brings into question our most ‘successful’ theory/model in Cosmology. Here we have an observation of our Universe that is not explained by our ‘magnum opus’… so can we really claim the theory to be successful anymore?

In the next section, I will summarise what this theory is, and give some more details on what these disagreeing measurements are.

Level 1: $\Lambda \text{CDM}$, The Expanding Universe, and Introducing the Hubble Constant and Tension

So, what is this overarching theory that has been brought into question? We call it $\Lambda \text{CDM}$ where ‘CDM’ stands for Cold Dark Matter. Let’s briskly skip over what this means for now and leave the explanation to a later level. This model allows us to make a vast number of predictions about what we ought to see based on some assumptions – more on this later – when we make our observations. It has had unprecedented success in Cosmology, with it well explaining many phenomena we have seen in the Universe. It is by far the most common such model used in research today.

When an ambulance whizzes past you, sirens on, you hear the pitch of the sirens drop off as it moves further away. This is called the Doppler shift. A good example, using a car horn can be found here. Sound is a type of wave; that is, it moves from one place to another at a finite speed, from source to observer. Interestingly, light is also a wave and also has a finite, though very very fast, speed and can thus also have its ‘pitch’ (from hereon called its ‘frequency’) lowered if the source is moving away from us (the observer).

We systematically see this lower-than-expected frequency of light from a wide range of sources far away in the Universe. This informs us that the Universe is expanding. Try not to worry about the question ‘what is the Universe expanding in to?", it has no easy intuitive resolution. Think instead that the distances between things in the Universe is growing over time like between two dots on the surface of a balloon you are inflating… just without the room space you’re inflating it in…

The next natural question to ask is at what rate is the Universe currently expanding?

For now let us just say that we measure this using some value called the Hubble constant, which we denote with the symbol $H_0$ and is named after the astronomer Edwin Hubble.

$\Lambda \text{CDM}$ assumes that any measurement made of this constant is, as hinted by the name, the same value as any other measurement. Observations of this constant from looking at ‘relics’ in the Universe which have different ages give different values depending on the age (we will formalise what we mean here at a later point). Thus these observations are not predicted, nor fit within the framework of the $\Lambda \text{CDM}$ model.

Before one jumps to the conclusion that this is the downfall of the model, it is worth pondering if this is just an observational fault. Did we just mess up in measuring the constant? This is still a possibility, but as we take more and more measurements it becomes ever less viable as an explanation. Thus, the possibility of new physics, of a modification to $\Lambda \text{CDM}$ becomes more plausible. This is what I will be actively working on.

In the next section, I will formalise what we mean by these different types of measurements and explain how we broadly classify ‘age’ in the universe.
